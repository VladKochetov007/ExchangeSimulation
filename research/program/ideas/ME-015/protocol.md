## Question, contrast and identifying limits

Under the same finite ABC/USD maker/taker/round-trip roster, does permitting a maker to use a recent actor-local last two-sided snapshot when its latest local book is one-sided or empty increase the fraction of the registered `[10,55 min)` measurement window for which the **public** book is two-sided? The primary three-seed contrast is the treatment-minus-control difference in this fraction for the all-pure-maker roster P at a 0.1-ABC quote size. Let `Q = two_sided_public_ns / 2,700,000,000,000 ns`. Report all three paired `Q_on - Q_off` values, their median and range. There is no success threshold, equivalence margin, or positive-effect requirement. All-A and mirrored mixed-roster contrasts are secondary; do not pool them with P as nine or twelve independent replications.

This is a *development* test of a particular information/quoting mechanism, selected after r1's book extinction. The concurrent off/on comparison can identify the effect of the new finite-reference rule under the fixed successor candidate, not prove that makers in general restore liquidity. Equal seed labels align the named random-taker streams but do not make all endogenous counterparty paths identical after intervention. No empirical-market or equilibrium claim follows.

## Reference rule and anti-backstop contract

Each maker sees only its own gateway-delivered, processed ABC/USD snapshots. On a two-sided snapshot with `best_bid > 0` and `best_ask > best_bid`, it records the integer midpoint, source timestamp and sequence. A normal latest two-sided snapshot remains the immediate quote reference under the existing rule. If the **latest processed** snapshot is one-sided or empty, the treatment may instead use the last delivered two-sided midpoint only when `0 <= decision_time - reference_source_time < 15 simulated seconds`. Exactly 15 seconds is expired. This source-time clock includes publication and transport age; actor receipt is separately recorded. A crossed/locked snapshot is not a fallback opportunity. No prior delivered two-sided snapshot, an expired reference, a future timestamp or a mismatched sequence means no fallback. The control has fallback disabled, with the same policy/lifecycle and live-book rule.

The 15-second bound is fixed from three 5-second maker decision intervals and the declared 1-second local market-data delay, **not** selected to match any ME-015 outcome. It is an explicitly post-r1 development choice, not untouched validation. Reference selection changes neither the pure fixed-spread formula nor the AS-style inventory/variance formula; both receive the selected actor-local midpoint and retain their existing risk horizon, parameter values, rounding and quote size. Both roster types receive the same option. A reference is a belief, not a venue mark: terminal `P_T` remains the strict current public two-sided midpoint. It cannot be used to value wealth or to certify market survival.

There is no quote obligation. The maker can withhold one or both sides due to quote-rule domain, finite pending-inclusive working limits, finite base/cash, cancel/response waiting, post-only crossing/rejection, or a missing/expired reference. No seed-actor refill, external fair-value feed, direct book edit, forced recapitalization, guaranteed depth or horizon-end liquidity operation is permitted. The existing finite seed-once order remains exactly as in r1. Track if treatment depth consists mostly of mutually self-referenced maker quotes and whether the public price drifts without an external anchor; survival alone would not establish informative price discovery.
