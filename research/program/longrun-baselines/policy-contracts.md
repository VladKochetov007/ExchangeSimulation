# Policy, actor and deployment contracts (planning; no run authority)

Source baseline: `ef61b4cb6e0124aa797f281e379ab143e847646e`. These are candidate contracts, not assertions that every named policy is implemented or profitable. The existing [catalogue](../policy-catalogue.md) retains stable S-IDs and historical links. New descriptions below do not upgrade those source statuses. Applicable units: ABC or CDF base units, USD quote units, bps = 1/10,000 of price, time in simulated seconds, cash and prices stored at the venue's declared fixed-point precision (`exchange/precision.go`: ABC-style base 1e8, USD 1e5). Every formula requires checked rounding to book tick/lot; an undefined positive-price ratio is unavailable, not zero.

## Common contract

Each policy receives only delivered public book/trade/funding/lifecycle messages and its own account/order responses before its decision frontier. A policy's book view is not an instantaneous venue oracle. An actor owns finite initial cash/base/collateral, a working-risk limit distinct from deposited wealth, a liability or mandate if any, and no implicit replenishment. Deployment supplies independent directed market-data, outbound and response delays plus decision period/phase and declared compute service; host wall time never enters market time. Venue rules and fee assets are separately configured. All policies must have observable no-action decisions when abstention affects the estimand. A class label never determines latency or resources.

E0 common deployment draft: 1-second delayed snapshots, orders and responses, 5-second maker ticks, equal timing across maker policies, one ABC/USD venue. E2 has separate venue-local accounts and clocks; no cross-venue funds magically move. An additional slow/fast deployment contrast is a later study with same policy/resource profiles swapped across actor slots and stable RNG namespaces. If service time exceeds a decision period, a future adapter must specify whether computation queues, coalesces, or skips; it may not read later messages mid-computation. Neither E0 nor E2 models nonzero compute service time.

The ordinary lifecycle is `receive -> evaluate -> submit/cancel -> admission/rejection -> partial/full fill or cancellation -> account and policy-state update -> next decision`. The hard position guard uses worst-case exposure including filled position, live orders and in-flight requests/cancellations, not only last acknowledged inventory. Deadline, parent mandate, margin, and inability to value/borrow may also force abstention or exit. Terminal mark and actual executable exit are distinct.

## Estimator and source-deviation rules

The AS quotation in D is a **programmed policy approximation**. [Avellaneda and Stoikov (2008)](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf) derive reservation-price and spread expressions under a finite-horizon utility problem, Brownian price assumption and distance-dependent execution intensity. Our actual price is an endogenous order-book outcome; actual fills obey queue priority, competing quotes, delay and finite depth. The existing code uses a rolling risk horizon, EWMA log-return variance and configurable `RelativeFillDecay`; E0 must either fit the latter from delivered past exposures with a prior or label it a fixed prior and test sensitivity separately. Do not say “optimal AS strategy.” InventorySkewBps is an explicit override and should be OFF for the first formula contrast unless preregistered otherwise. Estimate variance from a fixed-time delivered midpoint series, not bid/ask-alternating trades. A valid estimate requires its declared positive-price domain and sample count; never silently read a current exchange mark.

Imbalance E uses a declared number of levels, self-order inclusion policy and delivery lag. Momentum F and mean reversion G use different beliefs about past prices; H uses an external believed value. Their apparent performance could instead arise from imposed demand correlation, book bounce, periodic clocks, self-generated order flow or a valuation benchmark. Test each first with synthetic sign/no-action fixtures. These components are a library of candidate policies, not a mandate to run every combination or choose parameter values after seeing a profitable seed.
