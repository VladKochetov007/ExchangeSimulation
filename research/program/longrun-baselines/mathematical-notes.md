# Mathematical notes for the first repeated-maker ecology

Status: planning assumptions and falsifiable reductions, **not** a proof of the implemented market's stationarity. Source baseline `ef61b4cb6e0124aa797f281e379ab143e847646e`. See the [E0 draft](config-drafts/e0-repeated-spot.md), [policy contracts](policy-contracts.md), and [E2 draft](config-drafts/e2-two-venue-funding.md). Equations below distinguish exact accounting from a reduced stochastic model and from future empirical-game estimates.

## A. Exact event/account contracts

At logical time `t`, venue state contains price-time order queues `L_t`, admitted orders, cash/base/debt/collateral ledgers, fees and published market data. Actor `i` has cash `C_i`, risky base `B_i`, pending/live orders `O_i`, delivered information `I_i(t)` and policy state `Z_i(t)`. An action must be measurable with respect to the actor's delivered filtration `F_i(t)=σ{messages received by i no later than t, initial private assumptions, own RNG draws}`. The actor cannot condition on an undelivered future exchange state. Equal-time event ordering belongs to the deterministic runner contract, not to sorting output files afterward.

For an E0 spot fill of `q` ABC at `p` USD/ABC, let side `s=+1` for buyer and `−1` for seller. Ignoring explicit venue-specific fee-asset conversions for one line, `ΔB=s q`, `ΔC=−s p q−f_USD`. If a fee is paid in ABC, it changes `ΔB` instead. Asset and currency ledgers reconcile transfers plus declared fee/sink/source entries; **total marked wealth need not be conserved** because `p` can change. Use fixed-point, checked arithmetic and exact venue rounding in a Go reconstructor; the displayed formula uses human units for comprehension.

E0 maker wealth at terminal valid mark `P_T` is `W_i(T)=C_i(T)+B_i(T)P_T`; a passive initial-endowment benchmark at the same mark is `W_i^passive(T)=C_i(0)+B_i(0)P_T`. With no external transfers the trading increment is `G_i=(C_i(T)−C_i(0))+(B_i(T)−B_i(0))P_T`. This is fee-net if actual fee postings are already in the ledgers; do not subtract them twice. Report `C`, `B`, fees, matched realized cashflow, marked residual and hypothetical executable closeout separately. `G_i` is unavailable when the preregistered mark domain fails; do not substitute a stale book or assign zero. An executable terminal bid/ask liquidation estimate, if registered, is a different counterfactual and is not an actual trade.

The working inventory is `Q_i=B_i−B_i(0)` for spot E0. A strict prospective guard must bound the worst feasible `Q_i` after all live and pending buys and sells, including cancel requests not yet acknowledged. At the same time, admission may reject orders due to cash/base constraints before this policy guard binds. Report separate counts for policy-denied, risk-denied, venue-rejected and no-visible-depth decisions. A bounded quote at each instant does not bound cumulative inventory if orders keep filling.

E2 has venue-local spot `C^S,B^S`, perp collateral `C^P`, signed perp position `H`, debt and funding transfers. A funding event at actual eligible position `H_{t^-}` and mark `M_t` transfers approximately `−H_{t^-} M_t r_t` to the account for a positive long-pays-short rate, with the implementation's fixed-point and opposite residual settlement explicitly reconciled. Funding is a cash transfer, **not a book-price update**. Matched carry PnL needs entry/exit spot and perp fills, fees, actual intervening funding, financing and any residual closeout. A predicted funding rate is not a realized payment. Distinct N/S accounts cannot be netted without an explicit transfer transaction.

## B. Reduced dynamic model: useful hypotheses, not engine rules

Let `q_t` denote an individual maker's signed ABC inventory relative to its starting endowment. Suppose buy and sell fill counts conditional on the delivered book and quote policy are `N^b,N^a` with local intensities `λ_b(q_t,L_t), λ_a(q_t,L_t)` per second for fixed lot `ℓ` ABC. An approximate one-step relation is `dq≈ℓ(dN^b−dN^a)`. Under independent conditional Poisson fills, `E[dq|state]/dt≈ℓ(λ_b−λ_a)` and `Var[dq|state]/dt≈ℓ²(λ_b+λ_a)`. The *implemented* matcher neither samples these Poisson intensities nor guarantees independence; competition, queue depletion and correlated demand can violate both expressions. Estimate conditional fill/exposure rates from actual order-book evidence before using the approximation predictively.

For an AS-style maker, the programmed source control `r=F−q_norm γσ²τ`, with `q_norm=clip(q/Q_limit,−1,1)`, shifts both quotes down when `q>0` under `γ,σ²,τ>0`. The original paper's share inventory is not the same as this normalized source input. If a lower quote improves the sell hazard and reduces the buy hazard locally, the resulting fill drift can be approximated by `dq/dt≈−a q+u_t`, `a>0`, where `u_t` is exogenous-to-this-maker net order-flow pressure. The sign of `a` is **not guaranteed** by the formula alone; queue priority, stale information, finite inventory and competitors may reverse it. Under finite-variance white `u`, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck approximation would give bounded inventory variance proportional to noise variance divided by `2a`, but this is a conjecture to test, not a stationarity certificate. A pure fixed-distance maker with cap-only control can have much weaker interior `a` while still behaving prudently at its boundary. Its cap and finite resources must remain identical to AS in the comparison.

Price level is a separate state. A schematic response `dP/dt≈−b q+c u_t+η_t` has **no** restoring `−k(P−v)` term unless a value anchor or economically motivated inventory response genuinely supplies one. If every policy depends only on local price differences and quote distances, the system may be approximately translation-invariant: inventory can mean-revert while the absolute price level drifts. A quiet 45-minute trajectory cannot establish price stationarity. To study a declared private-value environment later, write `x=P−v`, `dq/dt=−a q+b x+u`, `dx/dt=−k x−d q+ξ`. Linear local stability without delay requires negative trace `−(a+k)<0` and positive determinant `ak+bd>0` for the assumed signs/closure. With delayed inventory feedback `q(t−δ)`, the characteristic relation becomes `(λ+a)(λ+k)+bd e^(−λδ)=0`; phase lag may change stability even when the zero-delay linearization is stable. None of `a,b,d,k,δ` is an engine constant or a fitted result at present.

Falsifiers and discriminating fixtures for future authorization:

1. In known-state fixtures, positive inventory must move an AS maker's reservation quote in the declared direction; if not, the programmed control is wrong. An identical delivered history with swapped actor IDs must not change its decision except through specified queue/tie effects.
2. Under the pinned E0 matched rosters, estimate the conditional inventory-drift relation in bins of actual information age, imbalance and cap headroom. If `a≤0` or the sign changes with competition, retain that result rather than widening the risk parameter after seeing it.
3. Perturb initial maker inventories and starting book price under a prospectively amended design. Observe whether inventory restores, whether absolute price returns toward a *declared* reference, and whether the response differs by phase/delay. These perturbations are **not** included in the 15-world E0 draft.
4. Compare one-sided-book duration, quote age, flow and inventory variance across pure/mixed rosters. Resource exhaustion or missing marks can censor estimates; report it, never drop those worlds silently.
5. For funding E2, compare phase-conditioned payment and basis paths; a 5- or 8-minute periodic pattern is externally forced by the funding clock, not an autonomous market cycle. Varying phase is a later explicit contrast, not an after-the-fact explanation.

The finite-horizon utility approximation used for the [Avellaneda–Stoikov quotation](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf) assumes an exogenous Brownian mid and a parametric distance–fill relation. Here `τ=600 s` is a rolling *policy risk horizon*, not time until simulator shutdown or a claim that the order book obeys Brownian dynamics. The original paper also discusses a stationary formulation, but the current source is not a proof of it in a competitive endogenous book. No matching rule or price path should enforce the reduced equations.

## C. Future empirical game, only after reliable payoffs

A candidate game has players/roles `i`, finite resource allocations `R_i`, policy alternatives `π_i`, deployment assignments `D_i` and venue rules `V`. The object is a population-conditioned payoff `U_i(π_i,π_{−i};R,D,V)` with uncertainty across independent worlds, not a pairwise matrix assumed to extrapolate to arbitrary rosters. Initial E0 fixes `R,D,V` and compares a few roster cells. It is **not** a Nash equilibrium experiment.

A later restricted best-response test would predefine a finite policy/resource class, estimate unilateral deviation gains with common-randomness checks, and report an uncertainty interval for restricted regret. An invasion test would introduce a rare challenger without secretly adding total resources; replacement and additive entry are different interventions. A response graph can be non-transitive without producing persistent capital cycles. Stable price-statistic distributions, stable capital shares after perturbation, restricted strategic equilibrium, metastability and funding-calendar periodicity are distinct claims. Explicit entry/exit/reinvestment rules and source/sink accounting belong to a later Regime C protocol. None is authorized or assumed by E0/E2.
