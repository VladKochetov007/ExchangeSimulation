# Causal ablations

Each arm states its prediction and kill criterion before the run, and reports
what happened including where the prediction was wrong.

## V-002 — what makes the second spot book run away

**Question.** CDF/USD rises by a factor of forty-five to forty-nine over
twenty-four hours, driven by its own market maker becoming the dominant net
taker buyer. Is the cause the absence of a participant who cares about the
book's level, or the maker's own inventory-skew rule?

**Arms.** 24 simulated hours, seeds 101 and 103, against the frozen baseline.

| arm | change |
|---|---|
| control | frozen baseline |
| A | price-elastic participants split between ABC/USD and CDF/USD |
| B | maker inventory skew switched off for every Stoikov quoter |

**Prediction, stated before running.** If the missing level-caring participant
is the cause, arm A stays inside 2× on every seed and arm B still runs away on
the seeds where the control does. If the quoting rule is the cause, the
reverse.

**Result.** CDF/USD terminal price divided by opening price:

| arm | seed 101 | seed 103 |
|---|---|---|
| control | **49.41×** | **45.00×** |
| A — elastic supplier on CDF/USD | 2.07× | 1.05× |
| B — inventory skew off | **1.00×** | **1.00×** |

**The prediction was wrong, and in an informative way.** It was framed as an
either/or and both arms suppress the runaway. The two are not competing
explanations; they are the two halves of one feedback loop. The inventory skew
is the amplifier — as the maker accumulates inventory its reservation price
shifts, and when the shift exceeds the half-spread its own requote crosses the
standing book, printing a new midpoint that moves the next reservation price
further in the same direction. The price-elastic participant is the damper: it
sells into a rise and buys into a fall, so the loop cannot run away while it is
present. Adding the missing damper stops the instability, and so does removing
the amplifier -- which is what the two arms actually did. The baseline has the
amplifier and lacks the damper; neither arm removed a damper from anything.

ABC/USD is unaffected in every arm (0.96× to 1.00×), which is what a book that
already has a damper should look like.

**Second effect, not predicted.** The cross book's collapse is also driven by
the skew. ABC/CDF ends at 0.15–0.17× in the control, 0.15–0.25× with the
elastic supplier added to CDF/USD, and 0.80× with the skew off. So V-001's
compounding triangular dislocation is downstream of the same amplifier rather
than of the cross book's own lack of an anchor.

**Confounds and limits.**

- Arm B switches the skew off at every venue and on every book, so it is not a
  clean intervention on CDF/USD alone. It is the closest lever the frozen
  configuration exposes without adding one, and it was labelled as suggestive
  before the run.
- Two seeds per arm. The effect sizes are large enough — 45× against 1.00× —
  that the direction is not in doubt, but the dispersion is not characterised.
- Removing the skew removes a real economic behaviour: a maker that does not
  skew against inventory carries unbounded risk. The arm shows what causes the
  instability, not what the fix should be.

**What this establishes.** The runaway is causally identified: it requires the
inventory-skew rule and the absence of level-caring demand together. It is a
property of the population's composition, not an accounting or matching defect.

# The remaining arms, pre-registered

Written before any of them was run. Configurations are generated by
`scratch/build_ablation_arms.py`, which copies the frozen baseline and applies
exactly one delta per arm; the frozen configuration itself is never edited.
Every run is pinned to one core (V-008), 24 simulated hours, seeds 101 and 103.

Each arm below states what is removed, what should happen if the mechanism is
doing the work attributed to it, and what result would kill that attribution.

## Cross-venue price agreement — `abl-own-mid-anchor`

**Removed.** The shared index: every maker anchors to its own book instead.

**Prediction.** Cross-venue dispersion of the ABC/USD midpoint widens by more
than an order of magnitude from its present ~7 bps, and the cross-venue
arbitrage cycle starts showing profitable instants where it currently shows
none.

**Kill criterion.** Dispersion within a factor of two of the control and the
cycle still never profitable — which would mean something other than the index
is holding the venues together, and V-004 is wrong about the mechanism.

## Basis and carry — `abl-basis-off`

**Removed.** Cash-and-carry desks on the perpetual and on the dated ladder.

**Prediction.** The perpetual basis widens and its mean reversion slows; the
dated basis stops converging into expiry. There is no explicit convergence
force in the model, so if convergence survives the removal, some other
mechanism is producing it and must be named.

**Kill criterion.** Basis distribution and convergence unchanged, which would
show the carry desks are not what closes the basis.

## Put-call parity — `abl-parity-off`

**Removed.** The parity desks.

**Prediction.** Parity residuals grow and stay open longer. Present residuals
never pay after fees (−41 to −43 bps), so the measurable quantity is the raw
residual before costs rather than the profitable share.

**Kill criterion.** Residual distribution unchanged.

## Triangular arbitrage — `abl-triangle-off`

**Removed.** The triangular desks.

**Prediction.** Little changes. The desk already fires the profitable direction
thousands of times without restraining the dislocation (V-001), so this arm is
a null control: it tests whether the audit can tell a mechanism that is present
but ineffective from one that is absent.

**Kill criterion.** A large change, which would mean the desk was doing more
than the V-001 measurement credited it with.

## Funding — `abl-funding-off`

**Removed.** Funding, by capping the rate at one basis point and stretching the
interval to a day.

**Prediction.** The perpetual's anchoring to spot weakens and its premium
distribution widens and drifts, since funding is the only mechanism tying the
two together once the carry desks are priced out.

**Kill criterion.** Premium unchanged, which would mean the anchoring comes
from the makers' shared index rather than from funding — a shared upstream
cause of exactly the kind section 12 of the plan warns about.

## Option delta hedging — `abl-delta-hedge-off`

**Removed.** Dealer hedging in the underlying.

**Prediction.** Option flow stops transmitting into the underlying: the
correlation between option volume and underlying trading falls, and dealer
delta grows without bound instead of being returned to the market.

**Kill criterion.** Transmission unchanged, which would mean the observed
option-to-underlying flow comes from somewhere other than hedging.

## Vanna-volga desks — `abl-vanna-volga-off`

**Removed.** The desks that trade options to flatten vega, vanna and volga.

**Prediction.** Dealer second-order exposures grow, and cross-strike relative
pricing loses whatever structure the desks impose on it. This arm is also part
of section 10: any smile that survives it is not coming from vanna-volga
behaviour.

**Kill criterion.** Exposures and surface unchanged.

## Option value takers — `abl-option-value-takers-off`

**Removed.** The participants holding SABR views.

**Prediction.** This is the decisive arm for section 10. If the smile is
inherited from the SABR priors, it flattens materially here. If it survives, it
is produced by dealer inventory and hedging rather than by anyone's assumed
surface.

**Kill criterion.** No interpretation is possible if the chain stops trading
altogether, since these are a large share of option demand; that outcome would
be recorded as inconclusive rather than as evidence either way.

## Latency ×10 — `abl-latency-x10`

**Removed.** Nothing; every configured link is made ten times slower.

**Prediction.** Cross-venue dispersion widens, arbitrage opportunities last
longer, and the makers' adverse selection rises because their quotes are stale
for longer.

**Kill criterion.** No measurable change, which would mean latency is not
binding anywhere in this population and the whole latency layer is decorative.

## Liquidation — the arm that cannot be written yet

Section 11 asks for a liquidation-off arm. It cannot be run meaningfully
because liquidation never fires in the baseline (V-005): switching off a
mechanism that never executes is guaranteed to change nothing. The stress arm
in `research/validation-audit.md` has to fire it first, and only then does an
ablation of it mean anything.

# Verdicts

Scored against the preregistration text above, which is unchanged. Two seeds
per arm, paired seed-to-seed against the deterministic baseline (`det_101`,
`det_103`). Two treatment seeds cannot establish robustness, so **SUPPORTED
here means screening-stage causal evidence only.**

## A measurement limitation that shapes three verdicts

The ablation runs' raw event logs were deleted after seven metrics were
extracted from each, to keep the campaign inside the disk budget. That decision
was made before this scoring pass was planned in detail, and it was wrong:
dealer greeks, implied-volatility structure, the frozen mean-reversion
estimator, hedge-tagged flow correlation and realized latency are **not
recoverable** from what survives. Three arms are therefore NOT IDENTIFIED for
want of measurement rather than for want of an effect, and one more is scored
on a proxy. The baseline's raw logs were retained; the arms' were not, which is
exactly the wrong way round for paired comparison.

Nothing below substitutes a metric that happened to be available for one that
was preregistered. Where the preregistered quantity is missing, the verdict is
NOT IDENTIFIED and the available numbers are labelled as diagnostics.

### Diagnostics, clearly secondary

These did not enter any verdict.

- **delta-hedge-off:** dealer gross quantity −18% (1.201e12 → 9.838e11 on 101);
  spot-maker fills −7%; exploitable carry frequency +51% (0.138 → 0.209).
  Consistent with hedging flow having been a real component of underlying
  volume, but the preregistered transmission test was not run.
- **vanna-volga-off:** parity-desk activity −93% (79,311 → 5,774 and
  95,498 → 5,816); dealer gross quantity −46%. Removing the second-order desk
  changes first-order option pricing enough to starve the parity desk. Large,
  unpredicted, and unexplained by anything measured here.
- **option-value-takers-off:** viable windows −31% / −30% (3,873 → 2,664 and
  3,912 → 2,744) while the chain keeps trading. Book quality falls materially
  without the SABR-view participants even though volume survives.

## Summary

- **SUPPORTED (screening): 1** — own-mid-anchor
- **MIXED: 3** — basis-off, parity-off, latency-x10
- **FALSIFIED: 2** — triangle-off, funding-off
- **NOT IDENTIFIED: 3** — delta-hedge-off, vanna-volga-off, option-value-takers-off

Note the seed dispersion in the controls themselves: profitable cross-venue
instants differ by 15× between seed 101 and 103 (10,506 against 714), and the
parity residual changes sign (+28.05 against −4.74). Any arm whose effect is
smaller than that is not measurable with two seeds, which is part of why
funding-off and basis-off are hard to read.
